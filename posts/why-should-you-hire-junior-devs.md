I remember starting out at my first job as a full time programmer. I was incredibly anxious and dealt with a fair amount of imposter syndrome. I knew what I had gathered from college and my own learnings, but I didn't have the experience needed to understand how real world applications were built. I was excited to learn and loved solving problems but I lacked context.  

The people who had been in the trenches above me were willing to take time and effort to show me how things were done, and were willing to demonstrate how to build things the right way. If not for their willingness to teach me, and my willingness to learn, I never would have become the engineer I am today. It's my belief  

I'm not arguing that you should hire junior developers because it's a moral thing to do - although it is the right thing toåß do and we should absolutely invest in the next generation of engineers. I'm arguing that it will make your development team and culture as a whole stronger.
